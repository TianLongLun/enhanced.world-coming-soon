A2 · GOVERNANCE — FORMAT COMMITTEE
A2 The Format Committee (FC) SUPREME GOVERNING BODY · FINAL APPEAL · INTEGRITY
#a-fc +

The Format Committee is the supreme governing body ensuring technical evolution and integrity of the ECB format. It is the final appeal and decision-making instance on all format, scoring and eligibility matters.

Composition

  • Representative Members: Organizers, co-creators and strategic partners.
  • Person Members: Invited exceptional individuals. Once appointed, members self-renew every 2 years through an independent internal process.
  • Judicial & Community Representation: 3 Representatives of the Judges and 2 Representatives of the Community — ensuring oversight of proceedings and voicing community interests.

FC Authority Includes

  • Final decisions on format evolution, scenario rules and scoring modifications.
  • Approval of custom privacy tier changes and AI licensing decisions.
  • Review of formal objections submitted by Team Responsible Entities.
  • Waiving of fees in exceptional circumstances.

A6 Future Plans — Cyber-Physical Security & Integrity System (C-P SIS ) ISI · CIB · AMS/N · EEAC
#a-cpsis +

Future editions of ECB will introduce mandatory pre-qualification and eliminations for half of all participation slots. The baseline event format is part of a multi-purpose system designed for global cybersecurity development.

C-P SIS | Recurring Standards & Ultimate Tests Family

  • In-Flight System Integrity: Breach/Detection (ISI B/D) — rapid-cycle penetration and unauthorized modification of operational hardware/software, from consumer electronics to physical autonomous AI systems.
  • Cross-Domain Infrastructure Breach (CIB) — goal-oriented penetration of complex commercial-grade networks (Smart Environments) to achieve kinetic effects or data exfiltration.
  • Autonomous Machine Survival/Neutralization (AM - S/N) — evaluation of multi-tier resistance and neutralization strategies, from electronic warfare and reprogramming to physical immobilization.
  • Extreme Environment Autonomy Competitive Format (EEAC) — autonomous machine mission-integrity testing across hostile or exotic environments: sub-surface, aerial, aquatic and high-vacuum simulations.

▸ WHY ECB — OPERATIONAL BENCHMARK · VALUE · DIFFERENTIATION
V1 ECB vs Traditional Security Testing PENTESTS · RED TEAMS · CTFs · VENDOR BENCHMARKS
#comparison +

Security leaders currently rely on penetration tests, red team engagements, tabletop exercises, and CTF competitions to evaluate cyber readiness. Each provides useful insights, but they measure isolated fragments of security capability and rarely simulate sustained operational defense under real pressure.

ECB fills this measurement gap by combining real infrastructure defense, multi-day attack pressure, human-AI collaboration analysis, and measurable operational outcomes in one framework.

Comparison Matrix

Method Duration Cost Environment What It Measures Limitations
CTF Competition 24–48 hours Free–low Challenge-based Offensive skill, exploit discovery Little operational realism
Tabletop Exercise 1 day $15k–$50k Discussion-based Decision-making process No real infrastructure
Penetration Test 1–4 weeks $15k–$40k Controlled infrastructure Vulnerability discovery Limited operational context
Red Team 2–6 weeks $25k–$85k Real infrastructure Attack success paths Usually one-directional
Vendor Benchmark Varies Vendor-funded Lab environment Product performance Vendor bias
ECB Benchmark ~7 days Infrastructure + team time Real infrastructure under attack Breach + detection + availability + SOC operations Requires committed team participation

Metrics Captured in ECB

Defensive performance:

  • Mean Time to Detect (MTTD)
  • Mean Time to Recovery (MTTR)
  • Service uptime during attack
  • Detection coverage of attack attempts
  • Monitoring accuracy

behavioural SOC metrics:

  • Decision latency under alert floods
  • Team coordination patterns
  • Tool-switching costs between SIEM / EDR / forensics
  • Fatigue-driven response degradation

Offensive performance:

  • Successful breaches
  • Stealth infiltration attempts
  • Data extraction attempts
  • Service disruption attempts

These data points help organizations understand how their security teams perform under sustained operational pressure.
