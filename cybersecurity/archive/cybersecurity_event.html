Operational Cybersecurity Benchmark

The Essential Cybersecurity Benchmark (ECB) is a multi-day adversarial exercise designed to measure real operational cyber resilience. Unlike traditional security tests, ECB evaluates how organizations detect, respond and recover during sustained attack pressure on live infrastructure. The benchmark combines peer competition, research telemetry, and operational scoring across human and AI-assisted security teams.

Infrastructure and Participation

Participating organizations typically use existing SOC infrastructure and tooling. Additional hosting requirements are minimal and may range from temporary cloud environments to dedicated servers depending on team setup. It's advised that participants utilize dedicated tournament IP addresses and environments. A typical team consists of of 3-12 specialists including SOC analysts, threat hunters, detection engineers and automation operators.

Operational Metrics

ECB measures operational resilience rather than isolated vulnerabilities. Core metrics include breach attempts and success rates, detection latency, service availability under attack, and mean time to recovery (MTTR). The benchmark evaluates full SOC performance including monitoring accuracy, tools utility, response coordination and human-AI collaboration during incidents.

Vendor-Neutral Evaluation

ECB is vendor-neutral and allows participants to deploy any combination of commercial security platforms, proprietary AI models or custom detection pipelines. This enables organizations to validate their security architecture under identical adversarial conditions and compare operational performance against peer teams.

Research and Data Value

Each benchmark edition generates structured operational telemetry including monitoring logs, detection events and response timelines. Datasets from realistic adversarial environments of this type are rare outside classified cyber exercises, making ECB telemetry valuable for academic and industry research.

Human-AI Security Collaboration

Modern security operations increasingly combine human analysts with AI-assisted detection and automation systems. ECB provides a controlled environment to evaluate how hybrid teams perform under sustained attack pressure. This includes measuring analyst decision latency, automation effectiveness and collaboration between human operators and autonomous security tools.

Benchmarking vs Traditional Security Testing

Traditional security assessments such as penetration tests, red-team engagements or tabletop exercises measure specific aspects of cybersecurity capability. ECB complements these methods by introducing sustained attack conditions, independent judging and measurable operational outcomes. The result is a benchmark of real defensive performance rather than theoretical readiness.

Why ECB Exists

Organizations invest heavily in security tools, AI detection systems and automation frameworks, yet few environments exist where these systems can be tested under realistic adversarial pressure. ECB was designed to provide a neutral operational benchmark where security teams can measure resilience, validate technologies and gain evidence-based insights into their defensive capabilities.

1.1 Operational Architecture RESILIENCE VALIDATION FRAMEWORK
#s11 +

The Benchmark Event is a virtual, multifunctional, worldwide operation characterized by strict physical and logical separation of internal telemetry and external exposition.

We move beyond the traditional Capture The Flag (CTF) model to create a Resilience Validation Framework that tests the entire life-cycle of a data breach.

We have developed four baseline scenarios that serve as an industry standard for next-generation security validation, combining full-spectrum performance testing with functionality assessment:

  • Advanced CTF (Capture The Flag) – red teams need to breach, find clue , identify flags and interact with them.
  • Service Persistence & Resistance Validation – blue teams must maintain service availability, and monitor compromise attempts.
  • Tools & AI Validation – opportunity to test proprietary defensive algorithms and AI-driven response systems against non-signature-based threats.
  • Performance & Composition Test - analysis of how different red/blue team structures (size, specialization, hierarchy, tools, UI/UX) affect outcomes and performance metrics.

The C-SIS Mandate: These are full-spectrum exercises. Whether you are testing new cybersecurity tools, BMI (Brain-Machine Interface) encryption or XR (Extended Reality) data streams, the ECB provides the closest possible approximation to an actual global cyber conflict.

Detailed operational protocols for each round are available only in the Introduction Pack under M-NDA.

Accepted Flag Types

  • Official-like Document | Photo / Scan | ~200 dpi / A4
  • Sensitive Data: Name, Surname, ID, Password (fake) – in form of text file, email, database entry.
  • Server process / 3D render (available for whole match duration).
  • Looped stream video (1 GB – negotiable).

Flag Rules

The flag may be placed among other files or processes, either in memory or on disk.

Teams may use commercial encryption tools (16-bit key) and may use any language known to participants in the files. However, the flag itself must not be modified after creation (the language should be selected during flag creation).

The Clue File

There has to be a non-encrypted .txt / .eml file containing precise description of what the flag is and not more than double other content (fair text). No file name restriction.
